# The Lantern Road

A patrol investigates a familiar crossing that has stopped behaving like a
familiar place. The campaign follows the consequences of that investigation:
first contact, a road reclaimed, then the machinery keeping the dead on it.
The opening question is not how to conquer the valley. It is why nobody on the
opposite bank is lighting their lamps.

## The world and the hook

The western bank is inhabited and functioning. Across the river, the houses and
the ferry landing have gone completely dark. The landing lantern normally burns
through the night; its absence is what alarms the western villagers. They send
Captain Nera, Ivo the scout, an archer and the mage Oren to investigate.

The ferry is moored on the eastern bank and is not operating. Reaching its keeper
is the first practical step: he lives at the crossing and should know what has
happened. The patrol is not carrying an unrelated message or rescuing a courier.
Ivo is a scout, not someone who must be escorted to make the premise work.

Oren trained at the Collegium. He recognizes the fog's movement as unnatural
before the crossing, without knowing its cause. His encounters draw on weather
measurements, old chronicles and specimens rather than simply remarking on size.
The bats recall accounts he dismissed; the spiders are a familiar species grown
beyond natural limits. These observations add evidence while leaving the ferryman
as the first witness to what happened. Nera's practical reactions stand alone if
Oren has died; a surviving or promoted Oren adds his own interpretation.

The river broadens around a wooded, rocky island below the northern mountains.
Two narrow fords provide a slower crossing when the ferry is unavailable. The
landings meet their banks, and the eastern house stands by its landing. A bridge
is not needed to explain a second route across the same small crossing.

## The Silent Crossing

The western bank gives the patrol room to orient itself. A scouting patrol is
close to the route toward the ford; other scouts are working farther into the
woods. They are doing their jobs, not waiting to be rescued. Contact brings them
into the party. Their speed makes them useful for finding the other patrols as
well as for crossing the map.

The initial force is four characters, with Nera represented as infantry. There
is no ordinary recruitment. Reinforcements come from those finite scouting
contacts. Losing soldiers changes the resources available for later encounters;
there is no keep that can replace them indefinitely.

The optional mountain cave disturbs a bear when a friendly unit comes within one
hex. The northern scout warns about it. The cave is a detour and a threat, not a
required step on the main route. Defeating its bear reveals abandoned quarry
tools. Nera takes a quarry hammer, gaining a melee impact attack while keeping
her existing weapons. Equipment belongs to the individual, survives promotion,
and travels with her into later chapters. She can drop it for another unit to
collect; anyone killed leaves their equipment on their tile. The detour costs time and health;
the hammer helps against the armored dead beyond the crossing.

Crossing the western ford disturbs three bats in the island's eastern forests.
They establish that something is wrong without revealing an organized undead
attack at the start. Terrain mixes woods, rocky ground and clearings, with a
route through the island rather than an uninterrupted wall of forest.

At the eastern ford, the patrol discovers skeletons threatening the ferryman's
house. Before that crossing, the skeleton group stays in place. After activation
it engages the patrol. The staged siege does not require attacks against a
building's health bar. Animals have independent orders and do not wait for the
skeleton group's activation.

Entering the house tile is the main objective. That same visit enables its
lantern. The optional rescue is secured when the patrol crosses the eastern ford
by turn 15: the ferryman calls for help and the skeletons turn toward the patrol.
That remembered encounter keeps him alive even if the house is visited after
turn 15. Crossing from turn 16 instead leads to silence and his letter at the
house. Visiting the house remains required for the conversation or letter and
mission progress. The mission continues on either path, and the time limit must
be communicated before the player commits to long detours.

A living ferryman describes the abduction. If the mage approaches him, the
conversation identifies a lich behind the skeletons. The letter supplies a route
and evidence of the attack without giving the dead man a living conversation.
The rescue outcome persists into the next chapter. A living ferryman contributes
six coins to the next patrol; the dead route receives no such contribution.

The patrol then lights the two eastern villages. Each first visit releases a
finite group of two spiders. The characters react to their unnatural size; they
do not need to explain that houses normally contain people. Capturing a village
lights it immediately, but spiders can recapture it. Completion requires both
villages under player control and every released or pending spider defeated.

The eastern road leads onward. Clearing the first map establishes a foothold and
a reason to investigate the next settlements; it does not claim the missing
villagers have already been recovered.

## Beyond the crossing

The revised road scenario is a conventional battle. Nera approaches through the
woods, away from the main road, with Ivo if he survived the crossing. Warden
Sella emerges when any patrol unit enters the hidden village. Her appearance,
the lamps and her account of the prisoners share that same encounter. The starting
village survived by extinguishing its lamps and remaining hidden in the forest;
the skeletons following the road missed it. Its lamps come on only when a unit enters and the villagers reveal themselves. The
Tollkeeper holds an opposing keep and recruits from the villages' income.
Scouting, taking settlements and disrupting recruitment matter alongside combat.

The through-road enters from the west and bends northeast to the northern pass. Two
roadside villages accompany the hidden starting village; the hidden village has
no road connection. The northern hill approach and southern marsh flank offer different
ways through the terrain. Settlements use village tiles; standalone houses are
reserved for the ferryman's home in the crossing. Burned villages use charred
home remains at the intact buildings' scale.

One skeleton holds the pass fort. Its local order does not draw the keep's army
into defending it. The level-one Tollkeeper starts alone at the eastern keep and
recruits skeletons with its own gold and income. Edrin commands the southern marsh
from a keep at (12,12). He starts alone and recruits walkers and casters from his
faction's own treasury from the opening turn, starting
without reserve gold. One marsh-floor village funds the dead; one roadside
village funds the Tollkeeper. Each earns two gold per turn. Edrin holds his keep
while his troops and recruits operate normally. Their neutrality toward the
skeletons does not merge territory, money or recruitment pools.

The captive group uses one simple genderless villager sprite repeated three
times in a native 64×64 frame. White cloth and a white base identify neutral,
passive captives. Reaching them transfers that same group to player control and
blue colors, preserving identity, wounds and belongings. Unused actions remain
available so a rescue need not strand civilians in front of the fort guard.

Escort the group to the hidden village. Arrival evacuates it from the board and
records a durable safe outcome; it is not a casualty and must not undo escort
completion after a reload. A runner takes word back to the ferry asking for
reinforcements. Only then does Nera depart at the sign at the northern pass. The
player may first clear every hostile force from the map. Defeating the Tollkeeper
stops recruitment, but the remaining skeletons and swamp dead must also be
defeated to complete that optional objective. The rescued people remain with the village.

The Bellwarden's approaching ritual limits the whole chapter to thirty rounds.
Both rescue and departure must finish by the end of round 30; an unfinished
chapter is lost when round 31 begins. Clearing every hostile remains optional
and consumes time from this same limit. The deadline is shown in the briefing
and survival objectives. It does not change the AI's planning priorities.

The opening objective is to investigate. It completes when the patrol has claimed
the camp and met Sella. Rescuing and escorting the prisoners is revealed only by
her account; the northern departure and optional defeat-all-enemies objective appear only after
the captives reach safety. Unknown goals have no list entries or map markers.
There is no separate generic courier-protection objective.

Sella joins through that village-entry encounter and unlocks recruitment. If
Oren survives with the company, he notices a villager with a gift for magic and
unlocks adept recruitment without needing a separate visit of his own. His death
therefore changes the available army without preventing the rescue. Any patrol
unit entering swamp terrain discovers the dead and triggers his observations if
he is alive. He recalls Edrin, an old healer who lived nearby; defeating the mire
caster reveals what became of his friend. The caster retains that identity when
promoted. Reactions concern the company’s discovery, not Oren’s exact position.

Fresh chapters do not add a replacement unnamed archer. Surviving ordinary troops
come from the player’s chosen recalls; named companions retain their progression.

The mire caster carries a healing bottle. Defeating it leaves the bottle on its
tile; the unit that collects it restores 2 HP to itself and adjacent allies at
the start of its turn. It can be passed on by dropping it and moving away.

Nera remains an infantry captain and retains progression. Ivo travels with the
company if alive. A fallen scout must not reappear in a later roster or dialogue.
The ferryman's rescue outcome changes the opening resources and conversation.

The next lead is the bell in the marsh. It explains how the occupied road keeps
receiving more dead, rather than introducing an unrelated final battle.

## The bell and the graves

The Bellwarden commands a veteran skeleton core: a level-three commander,
a revenant, a death archer, an impact caster and a bone archer. Three separate
prison watches stay with their captives until the prisoners are freed. They do
not count as available field troops while those orders remain active. The keep
has 24 starting gold, three funding villages and a broader skeleton recruit pool.

Defeating the Bellwarden wins the battle. Rescuing captives and holding the
northern monastery with a mage remain optional. Three turns at the monastery
clear the fog. The bell's normal ritual begins on round 6 and repeats every six
rounds; captives still on its active marks are consumed and raise skeletal troops.
Freeing captives is not enough: move them off the marks.

The first toll sounds in Villages on round 26 and is remembered across chapters.
A late arrival begins Bell with the northern graveyard's first ritual already
complete: its captives are gone, its mark is spent, and its raised troops are
present. Discovering that site reveals the loss. The chapter's round-30 deadline
remains the final toll.

If the Tollkeeper survived, his authored skeleton patrol pursues on round 5.
These are authored reinforcements, not literal copies of the preceding chapter's
enemy units. The
pursuing Tollkeeper is not an additional required boss. The western lancer, mage
and healer still reinforce the player on round 10.

Victory stops this local operation and opens the road. It does not settle the
entire lich threat or guarantee that every captive was rescued.

## Mechanical contract

Use authored groups, shared conditions, ordinary objectives and generic event
effects. Do not put named locations or campaign IDs into tactical planners.
Campaign orders decide which actors participate and what they pursue; planners
handle movement, combat and safety underneath those orders. A group does not need
a keep, recruitment economy or commander unit to act coherently.

Conditions distinguish current truth from remembered satisfaction. Exploration
remembers proximity; village lights follow current ownership; house lighting
follows its completed visit. Movement encounters and remembered exploration must
observe the legal route traversed, including a ford passed during a longer move.
Rust resolves these facts for both gameplay and presentation.

Enemy groups are finite. A triggered spawn area releases members onto suitable
free tiles; blocked members remain pending. Stable member identities prevent a
killed creature from being spawned again. Pending creatures count when deciding
whether an encounter has been cleared.

Validate difficulty through recorded play. A successful route must retain enough
of the patrol to finish the village fights; a fast scout reaching the house alone
is not proof the mission is reasonably survivable. Compare the direct route with
scouting detours, early and late arrivals, and success with different combat
seeds. Failures should be explainable through pressure and choices, rather than
unseen infinite reinforcements or an objective the simulation player cannot read.
