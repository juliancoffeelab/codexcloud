# Green Accord: story and mission contract

The village council hired an Overseer to supply timber for repairs and manage
the river crossing. His charter permits ordinary woodland harvesting, not the
sacred groves where Alder's people live. He commands paid charter guards;
Captain Dain leads one of their patrols.

The Overseer diverts the workers' wages. When they demand their pay and refuse
to cut the groves, he delivers them to the Rootbinder. The necromancer kills
them and binds their bodies into tireless logging crews. The Overseer gets
unpaid labor; the Rootbinder gets bodies and a protected foothold by the river.
They blame the disappearances on Alder's pack. Dain believes that lie when
the campaign opens. Lark can read memories held by living roots, but cannot do
so while the grove is being cut or while she is driven out of it.

## 1. The cutting camps

Capture both logging forts, which hold the tools and carts keeping the crews
supplied, and recover the missing workers' pay ledger from the northern post.
Owning a cutting marker alone does not end the mission. Taking the camps stops
the operation; the ledger contradicts the Overseer's account of the workers.

Dain's company has fought the pack, and casualties are acknowledged. Seeing
his officer's records gives him a reason to investigate rather than suddenly
declare friendship. He attends a vigil under a personal truce. If he dies,
the pack needs evidence the council can hear without him. His death remains
recorded; no subsequent mission resurrects him.

## 2. The grove's memory

Lark must occupy the central grove through three full enemy turns. Leaving or
losing it resets her work. Dain is a visible, playable ally when alive: he
protects the investigation he agreed to attend. The truce covers his patrol,
not the Overseer's other charter guards. The Overseer dismisses Dain and orders
those guards to destroy the evidence before it reaches the council.

The vigil reveals the withheld wages, murder and binding. Lark carries a living
branch containing the workers' memories. Dain can recognize the officer's seal
and corroborate their orders; if absent, the branch still carries the evidence.
The northern post holds guard supplies that can be recovered during the vigil.

## 3. The council across the river

The council is assembling at the northern village. The Rootbinder bars the
crossing to keep the evidence away from the workers' families and preserve his
source of bodies. Capture the bridge fort so households have a safe crossing,
and escort Lark to the meeting alive. Killing the necromancer cannot replace
these objectives. Tamsin, a village adept, joins here to help against the bound
dead; she is not resurrected from an unrecorded death in the vigil.

The council hears the branch and revokes the Overseer's timber and toll charter.
The villages and pack agree to protect the crossing and harvest ordinary woods
without cutting the sacred groves. The Rootbinder may remain a danger; the
ending does not claim an undefeated army vanished. Dain speaks only if he
survives. The victory is evidence delivered and the road held together.

## Implementation

Use ordinary maps, units, persistent event marks, control objectives and the
existing vigil/escort rules. Add an event objective for the ledger rather than
woodland-specific victory conditions in the engine. New campaigns use the
revised story; the save loader still recognizes old conversations. Earned
victories remain earned, and unfinished saves retain their units and progress.
