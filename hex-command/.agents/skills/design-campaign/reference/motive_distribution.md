# Motive distribution

Reference sample: **33 selected enemy pursuits across 19 distinct authored campaigns**. Each pursuit receives one motive kind and one role. An enemy can contribute several pursuits, including pursuits at different story stages. Counts describe the recorded pursuits, rather than the number of enemies or the probability that a campaign contains a motive. Use them as a rough reference, not a checklist or quota.

## Roles

- **Primary goal:** an end organizing the enemy's undertaking at that story stage.
- **Auxiliary goal:** an additional desired end alongside the primary pursuit.
- **Means:** something pursued because it enables another end.

These roles describe what the enemy fights to achieve. The leader's personality and the reasons individual soldiers participate are separate questions. Role assignments are interpretive; the relative priority of magical power and revenge is tentative in one sampled undertaking.

Raids / pillage is a generation-facing adaptation. Its counts are inherited weights, not a fresh tally of raids.

## Reading the distribution

- **How common is a kind?** Divide its row total by 33. Conquest/subjugation accounts for 8/33, about 24% of recorded pursuits.
- **How is a kind used?** Divide its cells by its row total. Four of five entries assigned to raids/pillage (80%) serve as means.
- **What kinds serve a role?** Divide by the column total. Five of 24 primary goals (about 21%) involve conquest/subjugation.

Row percentages sum to 100% across roles. Column percentages sum to 100% across kinds. These are different normalizations of the same counts.

## Kind distinctions

- **Conquer / subjugate:** gain territory or control over peoples.
- **Retain rule:** preserve existing authority or dominion against challengers.
- **Seize leadership:** take a throne or leadership of a faction.
- **Acquire wealth:** obtain money, loot or valuable possessions.
- **Magical power / knowledge:** obtain magical capability, knowledge or an artifact.
- **Revenge against someone:** retaliate for earlier harm; the target need not be the protagonist or someone responsible for the harm.
- **Defend people / possessions:** protect lives, territory or holdings against intrusion or attack, including aggression by the protagonist.
- **Raids / pillage:** attack settlements, strongholds or traveling groups to take valuables, seize prizes or weaken enemy holdings.

A kind does not determine its role. Wealth can be an end or finance a war; magical power can be a prize or enable conquest; raids can obtain either or weaken an enemy. The purpose established in the story determines the role. A zero means no recorded example in this sample, not an invalid combination.
