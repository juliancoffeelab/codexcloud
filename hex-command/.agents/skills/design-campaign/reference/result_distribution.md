# Finishing result distribution

Reference sample: **250 battle chapters** in nineteen story families. **200** have one finishing result; **50** have alternative finishing routes, contributing **103** alternative results. These are a working classification of the existing source-cited chapter records, with targeted ending checks. Primary result categories are interpretations of recorded achievements.

## Roles

- **Main:** the primary result of completing the chapter’s finishing goal, including a conjunction of required parts. Earlier tasks do not add finishing results.
- **One of alternatives:** the primary result of one independently sufficient finishing route. Each route contributes one result, even when several routes share the same broad category. A route requiring several parts still contributes one result.

Intermediate and optional developments stay in the event data. Optional rewards, ordinary defeat endings, eligible-unit substitutions and equivalent script triggers do not create alternative finishing results. A source-authored soft-failure continuation explicitly marked victory is retained as a setback, with that condition preserved in the research records.

## Kind distinctions

- **Safety:** people or the company survive or are brought out of danger, without claiming successful defense of a location.
- **Defense:** a settlement, camp or position is successfully defended or relieved.
- **Passage:** a usable way onward is opened/secured, or a route/obstacle is traversed.
- **Arrival:** the company reaches a specified destination, such as a sanctuary, city, refuge or facility. Permission to proceed alone is passage, not arrival.

Choose the primary achieved change from the recorded ending. The finishing action alone does not determine the result: combat can enable arrival, and movement can win passage.

## Use

Interpret the sampler's supplied results in their finishing-route contexts. These marginal counts describe the reference sample; they are not instructions to replace the sampler's objective-conditioned results with separate draws. Ground each supplied result in what this battle changes and what the player gains. Different destinations, beneficiaries or consequences can share a broad category.

For example, defeating an army and holding until relief can both achieve safety. Delivering commission materials and clearing their road instead achieve different results: assets received versus passage secured. A delaying force can buy preparation time even when its defenders die.

Main-column shares divide by 200; alternative-column shares divide by 103. The 103 alternatives are results from 50 chapters, not 103 chapters. Counts are not an action–result transition model or a guarantee that an independently sampled objective and result fit.

## Scope and earlier counts

This 250-template sample excludes the duplicate classic campaign version and differs from the 272-template [objective task sample](objective_distribution.md). The earlier 29/272 alternative-chapter tally was incomplete. In the shared scope, it missed 23 chapters with finishing alternatives and counted one intermediate defense stage as an alternative ending. The revised finishing-route rate is 50/250 (20%); do not combine those rates as if they came from the same complete tally.

The ten formerly “route-dependent” primary records are resolved into concrete route results here. **Time / preparation gained** represents the delaying-action route; it does not claim the enemy was defeated. Source dialogue, conditions and continuation remain in the research records; the categories alone cannot express them.
