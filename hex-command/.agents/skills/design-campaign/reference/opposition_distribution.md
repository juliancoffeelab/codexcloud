# Opposition distribution

## Organizing opposition

- **Principal individual:** one person principally organizes the hostile undertaking, usually through a faction and subordinate commanders.
- **Regime:** a ruling system and its military machinery organize the opposition; the campaign confronts that machinery rather than necessarily reaching its sovereign.
- **Coalition:** several principal enemies cooperate against the protagonist.
- **Successive enemies:** distinct opponents organize different stretches of the campaign.
- **Independent opponents:** separate enemy factions pursue their own undertakings, without a common leader or coordinated plan. Their activities intersect with the protagonist’s undertaking.
- **External and personal conflict:** external opponents combine with conflicts generated by the protagonist's choices, values or relationships.

These are working summaries of the dominant structure. The rows distinguish what organizes each campaign, rather than defining mutually exclusive kinds of faction: an individual can rule a regime or lead a coalition.

## Presentation

- **Open antagonist:** the hostile undertaking is apparent. The leader's name, history or full motives can still emerge later.
- **Concealed manipulator:** a central enemy influences events while concealing its hostility or presenting itself as an ally or trusted authority.
- **Hidden antagonist:** the hostile actor or undertaking is initially unknown and revealed as the story develops.
- **Mixed / changing:** the campaign moves between substantially different sources or roles of opposition, such as invasion followed by civil war, a mentor becoming an enemy, or predation giving way to confrontation with its consequences.
- **Unconnected mix:** independent opponents whose actions are not parts of one shared undertaking.

Organization and presentation are separate questions. A regime or coalition could be concealed or uncovered by investigation even though neither combination occurs in this sample. A zero records an absent combination, not an invalid design.

## Reading the distribution

- Divide a row total by 19 for the prevalence of that organizing structure: 11/19 campaigns center on one principal individual.
- Divide a cell by its row total for presentation within a structure: 7/11 individual-centered campaigns present an open antagonist.
- Divide a column total by 19 for overall presentation: 10/19 campaigns present open opposition.

Whether the protagonist is an aggressor is a separate question. So are environmental pressures, when characters learn the enemy's identity or motives, and whether their allegiance or original purpose changes. This matrix does not count those features.
