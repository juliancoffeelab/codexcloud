# Army design

Armies should tell stories through their mechanics. Their strengths, weaknesses
and available roles should follow their identity and lore.

## Faction identity

| Faction   | Character                                                                                                                                    |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Humans    | Flexible all-rounders with strong infantry. Their mages specialize in damage or healing.                                                     |
| Skeletons | A relatively cheap, fragile and dangerous army with piercing resistance. Their casters specialize in damage or stealing life for themselves. |
| Woodland  | Guardians of the forest. Thorn-bearing infantry punishes attackers, while support spreads that protection to nearby allies.                  |
| Merfolk   | Water specialists with distinct damage casters and healers.                                                                                  |

## Terrain and overall strength

Specialization should give an advantage at home while leaving a viable army
elsewhere. Aim for roughly 70% Woodland wins in forests and 80% Merfolk wins on
water. Woodland should be stronger than Merfolk on dry plains.

Humans and Skeletons should be roughly equally capable across opponents and
maps, particularly under Balanced and Situational. A fair direct matchup is desirable, but overall equality does not require
every matchup to be equal. Some stalemates between them are expected.

## Damage and usefulness

At comparable levels, the intended weapon damage order is:

**Infantry hitters > ranged damage units > defensive infantry ≈ support.**

Lower damage must buy another form of usefulness: defenders endure and protect,
support sustains or strengthens allies, and flankers gain mobility and objective
reach. Expensive heavy or mounted units can rival infantry hitters, with their
strength paid for through cost and other tradeoffs. Damage alone is not a ranking
of overall usefulness.

## Roles and tradeoffs

Defensive infantry earns its place through durability.

Cheap flankers trade combat strength for mobility, objective control and the
ability to punish exposed ranged units. Mounted units must be noticeably more
expensive; their cost can buy speed, durability or hitter-level damage. A mount
alone does not justify a stronger bow than an equivalent foot archer's.
Melee riders use the flank role at every promotion tier. Ranged riders retain
their separately authored roles.
Preserve meaningful cavalry use when tuning roster balance.

Woodland's Treants fill an expensive heavy-unit role in place of cavalry. They
combine strong protection with a meaningful blow, paying through price and slow
movement. Preserve their defensive identity when tuning their overall value.

## Equipment and movement

Equipment should behave intuitively: shields protect, heavy two-handed weapons
deliver strong individual blows, and horses provide speed. Shared armour should
give comparable protection to units wearing it. Trees should resist piercing
and be vulnerable to chopping blades.

Movement advantages need a reason in the lore, such as horses, wolves or scouts
trained for speed. Terrain familiarity can provide mobility without making
every unit fast everywhere.
Audit movement above three against the lore, counting terrain access and movement
costs toward mobility as well as the base move stat.

## Campaigns and validation

Campaign armies belong to their stories. Expand recruitment only when the lore
supports it; a faction's full roster does not automatically belong in every
campaign.

Assess damage alongside protection, support, mobility and objectives. Use
[recorded metrics](references/metrics.md) for evidence definitions, measurement
scopes and interpretation; use [replays](references/replays.md) when measuring
thorn deterrence. Attractive stat sheets alone cannot establish balance.
